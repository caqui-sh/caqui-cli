

# The 360° Dimensional Specification Framework: The Six Viewports

To comprehensively specify a single abstract information dimension, you do not plot it as a static coordinate. Instead, you look *through* all six operational perspectives, organized across three conceptual axes. Each perspective reveals a distinct, non-overlapping facet of the same dimensional space, tailored for high-level structural, semantic, and natural language logic.

---

## 🧭 The Entropy Axis (Informative vs. Inquisitive)

This axis governs **semantic variance**. It dictates how the information dimension modulates the density of valid meaning-states within the conceptual space.

### 1. The Informative Perspective (The Act of Semantic Convergence)

* **The Focus:** What is the mechanics of conceptual collapse or resolution within this dimension?
* **Specification Action:** Define how the dimension behaves as a selector. Specify the rules by which a field of multiple potential meanings converges into a single, immutable semantic constant, eliminating all alternative variances and dropping the dimension's local entropy to zero.

### 2. The Inquisitive Perspective (The Act of Semantic Divergence)

* **The Focus:** What is the topology of the question space or the field of alternatives?
* **Specification Action:** Define the parameter space of the unknown. Specify how the dimension acts as a generator of valid alternatives, mapping out the precise boundaries of curiosity, the dimensions of inquiry, and the conditions that must be satisfied before convergence can occur.

---

## 📐 The Geometric Axis (Primal vs. Dual)

This axis governs **structural representation**. It determines whether a dimension is specified by its internal contents or by the external boundaries that clip it into shape.

### 3. The Primal Perspective (The Interior Payload)

* **The Focus:** What are the atomic conceptual tokens or semantic invariants that occupy this space?
* **Specification Action:** Define the core substance. Specify the discrete semantic primitives, fundamental notions, or linguistic invariants that serve as the foundational "things" inhabiting the dimension. Map the irreducible conceptual identities that exist here before any rules or relationships are applied to them.

### 4. The Dual Perspective (The Negative Space Boundary)

* **The Focus:** What are the systemic pressures, exclusion rules, and conceptual limits that sculpt this dimension from the outside?
* **Specification Action:** Define the mold. Specify the semantic boundary conditions, structural filters, exclusion criteria, and regulatory constraints that dictate what is *impossible* or *prohibited* within this space. Define the dimension by its limits, capturing the exact shape of its containment.

---

## 🌐 The Locality Axis (Intrinsic vs. Yoneda Extrinsic)

This axis governs **identity derivation**. It determines whether an information dimension draws its definition from local properties or from global network topology.

### 5. The Intrinsic Perspective (The Isolated Self)

* **The Focus:** What is the semantic grammar of this dimension when isolated from the rest of the universe?
* **Specification Action:** Define the self-contained conceptual architecture. Specify the internal rules of generation, structural composition, and localized meaning invariants that remain completely stable and coherent without any reference or dependency on external categories.

### 6. The Yoneda Extrinsic Perspective (The Global Reflection)

* **The Focus:** How does this dimension manifest entirely through its global transformations and alignments?
* **Specification Action:** Define the global structural translations. Specify how this dimension projects onto, alters, or maps across every other abstract dimension in the macro-system. Define its identity entirely through its behavioral footprint and structural mappings into external conceptual spaces.

---

> ### Architectural Blueprint Execution
> 
> 
> When authoring technical documentation for an abstract dimension, dedicate a distinct section to each of these six viewports. Grouping them by their respective **Entropy**, **Geometric**, and **Locality** axes ensures the specification comprehensively maps the dimension's **behavior** (1 & 2), **geometry** (3 & 4), and **identity** (5 & 6).
